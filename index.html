Curriculum

A clear plan for the whole school year

The year is split into five themed blocks that follow the Dutch school calendar — each block runs between two school holidays. Siblings work on the same theme at their own level, and every block stands on its own, so children can join at any time.

Dutch core objectives

Built on the SLO core objectives for arts education (kerndoelen kunstzinnige oriëntatie 54–56): expressing ideas and feelings in images, reflecting on your own work and that of others, and discovering art and cultural heritage.

The creative process

Every class follows the creative-process model used in Dutch arts education: orient, explore, make and reflect — working with line, shape, colour, texture, space and composition.

Right for their age

Levels follow the recognised stages of children's drawing development (Lowenfeld): from playful symbols at 4–6, to storytelling and observation at 7–9, to realism and personal style at 10–14.

What a class looks like

Every class follows the same five steps, so children always know what's coming. Times shown are for a 45-minute class.

  1. 5 minWarm-up doodle

    A quick, playful drawing game to loosen up the hand.

    Get going
  2. 5 minLook & wonder

    We look at an artwork, a photo or a real object and talk about what we see.

    Orient
  3. 10 minTry it out

    Priya demonstrates a new skill step by step and everyone practises along.

    Explore
  4. 20 minMake it yours

    Each child creates their own drawing with the new skill — with personal tips from Priya.

    Make
  5. 5 minShow & share

    Children hold up their work, say what they're proud of and get a kind, useful tip.

    Reflect

The year plan

Choose a level to see what your child will draw and learn in each block.

Block 1 · Sep – Oct

Lines & Shapes

What we draw
  • Wiggly, zigzag and spiral lines — drawing to music
  • Circles, squares and triangles that become suns, houses and robots
  • A "shape monster" of your own
Learning goal

Holds the pencil with control and combines simple shapes into recognisable things.

Look at: Wassily Kandinsky's colourful circles

Block 2 · Nov – Dec

Colour Magic

What we draw
  • The three primary colours and mixing with crayons
  • Warm sunny pictures and cool snowy pictures
  • A Mondriaan picture with black lines and colour blocks
  • A drawing for Sinterklaas — ready for the shoe!
Learning goal

Knows the colours by name and chooses them on purpose.

Look at: Piet Mondriaan

Block 3 · Jan – Feb

Animals & Characters

What we draw
  • Animals made from shapes: cat, fish, bird, rabbit
  • Faces that feel happy, sad, surprised or sleepy
  • My own little character — with a name
Learning goal

Draws people and animals with a head, body and limbs, and shows how they feel.

Look at: Dick Bruna (nijntje / Miffy)

Block 4 · Mar – Apr

Places & Spaces

What we draw
  • My house and my street on a ground line
  • Sky at the top, ground at the bottom
  • A spring garden with flowers and bugs
  • Sunflowers with oil pastels
Learning goal

Places things in a picture with a ground line and sky, and adds texture.

Look at: Vincent van Gogh's Sunflowers

Block 5 · May – Jul

Stories & Portfolio

What we draw
  • A story in three pictures: beginning, middle, end
  • My family and me
  • A little picture book to take home
  • Choosing favourites for the online exhibition
Learning goal

Tells a story with pictures and talks about their own drawing.

Look at: Max Velthuijs (Frog / Kikker)

Block 1 · Sep – Oct

Lines & Shapes

What we draw
  • Line weight and pressure: light and dark, thick and thin
  • Finding the basic shapes in real objects
  • From flat shapes to 3D forms: cube, cylinder, sphere
  • Toys and fruit drawn from observation
Learning goal

Sees the basic shapes in real objects and draws them from observation.

Look at: Paul Klee — "taking a line for a walk"

Block 2 · Nov – Dec

Colour Magic

What we draw
  • The colour wheel and mixing secondary colours
  • Blending and layering with coloured pencils
  • Warm and cool colours to create a mood
  • A De Stijl composition and a winter or Sinterklaas card
Learning goal

Blends colours smoothly and chooses colours to create a feeling.

Look at: Piet Mondriaan & De Stijl

Block 3 · Jan – Feb

Animals & Characters

What we draw
  • Building animals step by step from construction shapes
  • Cartoon characters and facial expressions
  • Simple body proportions and poses
  • My pet (or dream pet) from a photo
Learning goal

Builds figures step by step and gives characters expression and movement.

Look at: Thé Tjong-Khing's picture books

Block 4 · Mar – Apr

Places & Spaces

What we draw
  • Overlap, size and the horizon line to show depth
  • A one-point-perspective street with Dutch canal houses
  • A landscape full of Van Gogh-style patterns and marks
Learning goal

Creates a sense of depth and space in a drawing.

Look at: Vincent van Gogh's The Starry Night

Block 5 · May – Jul

Stories & Portfolio

What we draw
  • Comic strips with panels and speech bubbles
  • An illustration of a favourite book scene
  • Building a sketchbook habit
  • Choosing and presenting work for the online exhibition
Learning goal

Plans a composition and gives kind, useful feedback to others.

Look at: classic comic strips (Marten Toonder, Hergé)

Block 1 · Sep – Oct

Observation & Tone

What we draw
  • Drawing from life: sighting and measuring proportions
  • Contour and blind-contour drawing
  • A five-step value scale
  • Hatching, cross-hatching and stippling
Learning goal

Draws from observation with believable proportions and a full range of tones.

Look at: Rembrandt's ink sketches

Block 2 · Nov – Dec

Colour Theory

What we draw
  • Complementary and analogous colour schemes
  • Layering and burnishing with coloured pencils
  • First steps with watercolour or markers
  • A still life with a limited palette
Learning goal

Plans a colour scheme and uses it with intention.

Look at: Johannes Vermeer and Van Gogh's colour contrasts

Block 3 · Jan – Feb

Figures, Faces & Characters

What we draw
  • Gesture drawing and figure proportions
  • Face proportions and a self-portrait
  • Character design with a turnaround sheet
  • Manga and cartoon styles
Learning goal

Draws faces and figures in proportion and designs an original character.

Look at: Rembrandt's self-portraits and manga artists

Block 4 · Mar – Apr

Perspective, Light & Illusion

What we draw
  • One- and two-point perspective: rooms, streets, canal houses
  • Light, form shadows and cast shadows
  • Tessellations and impossible shapes
Learning goal

Constructs convincing space and three-dimensional form.

Look at: M.C. Escher

Block 5 · May – Jul

Storytelling & Portfolio

What we draw
  • A comic page or mini zine
  • A personal project of their own choice
  • Building a portfolio and writing a short artist statement
  • Presenting work in the online exhibition
Learning goal

Completes a self-directed project and reflects on their own growth.

Look at: contemporary illustrators and graphic novels

How it works

From first message to first drawing

  1. Send a WhatsApp

    Tell Priya your child's age and which days suit you. You'll get the available places.

  2. Free trial class

    Your child joins a real class, free of charge. You'll receive a private link to join.

  3. Choose a plan

    Pay per class, take a 10-class card or join the monthly membership.

  4. Draw, share, grow

    A weekly live class, progress notes and an online exhibition at the end of the year.

What you need at home

  • A laptop or tablet with a camera and microphone (a phone works for older children)
  • A stable internet connection
  • A table with good light and a little space to spread out
  • We use a video-call app such as Zoom or Google Meet — you'll get a private link, and your child doesn't need an account
  • Tip: a simple phone or tablet stand makes it easy to show the drawing to the camera

Starter kit

  • A4 sketchbook (120 g/m² or more)
  • HB and 2B pencils (plus 4B for ages 10+)
  • Eraser and sharpener
  • A black fineliner
  • Coloured pencils (12–24)
  • Wax crayons or oil pastels
  • Washable felt-tip pens
  • Ages 10+: small watercolour set and brush

Everything is available at everyday shops such as Action or HEMA, or any hobby shop. No need for expensive supplies.

Meet your teacher

Hi, I'm Priya!

Drawing has always been my favourite way to tell stories, and I love sharing that with children. I started these online classes so that kids anywhere in the Netherlands can learn to draw in a small, friendly group — right from the kitchen table, without a long drive to a studio.

In my classes there's no such thing as a "wrong" drawing. We learn real skills step by step — lines, shapes, colour, perspective — and then every child makes it their own. Seeing a child realise "I can draw that!" is the best part of my week.

“Every child can draw. My job is to show them how much they already can — and help them take the next step.”
FAQ

Questions parents often ask

Which class is right for my child?

Age is the best guide: 4–6 years is Little Doodlers, 7–9 is Young Artists and 10–14 is Junior Studio. Every child is different, though — after the free trial class Priya can suggest moving up or down a level.

My child has never had drawing lessons. Is that OK?

Absolutely! Every block starts with the basics, and each child gets tips at their own level. Beginners and experienced young artists work happily side by side.

What language are the classes in?

Classes are taught in English, with lots of showing rather than telling. Drawing is a very visual language, so children who are still learning English follow along easily — and Dutch-speaking children are very welcome.

Does a parent need to be there?

For Little Doodlers (4–6), a parent or carer should stay nearby to help with setting up and to keep the camera pointed at the paper. Older children can join on their own once they know the routine.

What happens if we miss a class?

With a monthly membership, let Priya know at least 24 hours before and your child can join a catch-up class in another group at the same level (when there's space). With a 10-class card or pay-as-you-go, you only pay for the classes you attend.

Can my child join in the middle of the school year?

Yes. Each block stands on its own and starts with the basics of its theme, so children can join at the start of any block — or any week if there's space.

Are there classes during the school holidays?

Regular classes pause during the Dutch school holidays. Instead, there are themed 90-minute holiday workshops (€20) that anyone can join.

How do I pay and how do I cancel?

You can pay by Tikkie or bank transfer. The monthly membership is paid at the start of each month and can be cancelled at any time with one month's notice. There's no registration fee.

Will my child's work be shared online?

Only if you say yes. Drawings appear in the end-of-year online exhibition with first names only, and only with a parent's written consent. Classes are never recorded.
